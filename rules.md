Formal Specification of the Zero-Preserving Division Calculus

Status: Preliminary specification. Not peer reviewed.

1. Algebraic Carrier

Let

[
A=\mathbb R[I]/(I^2-I).
]

The distinguished element I satisfies

[
I^2=I.
]

Every algebraic element has a normal representation

[
a+bI,
\qquad a,b\in\mathbb R.
]

The carrier retains ordinary ring multiplication, including

[
0x=x0=0.
]

Therefore,

[
0I=I0=0.
]

---

2. Ordinary Algebraic Syntax

Ordinary algebraic expressions are generated by

[
E::=r\mid I\mid(E+E)\mid(E\cdot E),
]

where

[
r\in\mathbb R.
]

Algebraic expressions are evaluated in A.

---

3. Structured Division Syntax

Division is represented by

[
\operatorname{Div}(N;d_1,\ldots,d_n),
]

where:

- N is the numerator expression;
- d_1,\ldots,d_n are denominator factors.

The notation

[
\frac{N}{d_1\cdots d_n}
]

is shorthand for this structured representation.

The factorization of the denominator is retained until the division semantics have been applied.

---

4. Fundamental Singular Rules

The distinguished singular element is associated with division by zero:

[
\boxed{I:=1/0}
]

with

[
\boxed{I^2=I}
]

and

[
\boxed{0I=I0=0}.
]

Division by zero is defined by

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Div}(x;0)\to xI
}.
]

Equivalently,

[
\boxed{
x/0=xI
}.
]

---

5. Nonzero Division

For c\ne0,

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Div}(x;c)\to xc^{-1}
}.
]

Consequently,

[
\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)c=x
\qquad(c\ne0).
]

---

6. Factor-Sensitive Division

For c\ne0,

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Div}(x;0,c)
\to
xI c^{-1}
}.
]

Written conventionally:

[
\boxed{
\frac{x}{0c}=\frac{xI}{c}
}.
]

The algebraic rewrite

[
0c\to0
]

must not be performed before this division rule is applied.

---

7. Multiple Zero Factors

For denominator factors

[
d_1,\ldots,d_n,
]

define

[
Z={i:d_i=0}.
]

If Z\ne\varnothing,

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Div}(x;d_1,\ldots,d_n)
\to
xI^{|Z|}
\prod_{i\notin Z}d_i^{-1}
}.
]

Using idempotence,

[
I^{|Z|}=I,
]

so this normalizes to

[
\boxed{
xI\prod_{i\notin Z}d_i^{-1}
}.
]

---

8. General Division Rule

The complete intended rule is

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Div}(x;d_1,\ldots,d_n)
\to
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
x\prod_i d_i^{-1},
&
d_i\ne0\ \forall i,
\[1em]
\displaystyle
xI\prod_{d_i\ne0}d_i^{-1},
&
\exists i:d_i=0.
\end{cases}
}
]

---

9. Examples

One zero factor

[
\operatorname{Div}(x;0,c)
\to
xI/c.
]

Two zero factors

[
\operatorname{Div}(x;0,0,c)
\to
xI^2/c
\to
xI/c.
]

Three zero factors

[
\operatorname{Div}(x;0,0,0,c)
\to
xI^3/c
\to
xI/c.
]

No zero factors

[
\operatorname{Div}(x;a,b)
\to
x/(ab),
\qquad a,b\ne0.
]

---

10. Algebraic Normalization Rules

After structured division has been interpreted, ordinary algebraic normalization may be applied.

Important rules include

[
I^2\to I,
]

[
I^n\to I
\qquad(n\ge1),
]

and

[
0x\to0.
]

The ring identities of

[
\mathbb R[I]/(I^2-I)
]

may then be used.

---

11. Forbidden Rewrite

The following rewrite is valid in the underlying algebra:

[
0c=0.
]

However, it is not valid as a preprocessing step inside a structured division expression.

Therefore:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Div}(x;0,c)
\not\to
\operatorname{Div}(x;0)
}
]

before singular division evaluation.

Instead,

[
\operatorname{Div}(x;0,c)
\to
xI/c.
]

---

12. Equality

Two algebraic expressions may be equal in A without their corresponding structured division expressions being interchangeable.

For example,

[
0=0c
]

in A, but

[
\operatorname{Div}(1;0)
]

and

[
\operatorname{Div}(1;0,c)
]

need not be equivalent.

Thus the calculus distinguishes:

1. algebraic equality in A;
2. division-expression equivalence under the structured semantics.

A complete formal definition of division-expression equivalence remains an open specification task.

---

13. Cancellation

Cancellation is valid only under appropriate nonzero conditions.

For

[
c\ne0,
]

[
\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)c=x.
]

For c=0,

[
\left(\frac{x}{0}\right)0

xI0

0. 

]

Therefore,

[
\boxed{
\left(\frac{x}{0}\right)0=0
}.
]

Cancellation through zero is forbidden.

---

14. Singular Element

The element I is idempotent:

[
I^2=I.
]

It is also a zero divisor:

[
I(1-I)=0.
]

Therefore cancellation through I is forbidden unless separately justified.

---

15. Zero Divided by Zero

From

[
x/0=xI,
]

setting x=0 gives

[
\boxed{
0/0=0I=0
}.
]

---

16. Valid Derived Rules

The following are derivable:

[
\frac{a}{0}+\frac{b}{0}

\frac{a+b}{0}.
]

Proof:

[
aI+bI=(a+b)I.
]

Also,

[
\frac{a}{0}\frac{b}{0}

\frac{ab}{0}.
]

Proof:

[
(aI)(bI)

abI^2

abI.
]

---

17. Invalid General Rules

The following must not be treated as unrestricted identities:

[
\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)b=a
]

without b\ne0;

[
\frac{x}{0c}=\frac{x}{0};
]

cancellation through 0;

cancellation through I;

and ordinary fraction-combination identities involving singular denominators.

---

18. Nested Division

Nested division requires structured evaluation.

For example,

[
\frac{a/0}{b}
]

with b\ne0 becomes

[
\frac{aI}{b}.
]

The semantics of

[
\frac{a/0}{0}
]

must be defined through the structured syntax rather than by importing ordinary fraction identities.

---

19. Rewrite Contexts

The following order is intended:

1. Parse division expressions structurally.
2. Preserve denominator factorization.
3. Detect zero denominator factors.
4. Apply singular division rules.
5. Produce an algebraic expression in A.
6. Apply algebraic normalization.

Ordinary algebraic rewriting must not be allowed to destroy denominator factor information before step 4.

---

20. Intended Normal Form

A structured denominator should ultimately produce either:

Regular form

[
x\prod_i d_i^{-1},
]

when all denominator factors are nonzero;

or

Singular form

[
xI\prod_i d_i^{-1},
]

where all remaining d_i are nonzero.

Thus a normalized singular denominator contains at most one I contribution.

---

21. Open Formal Questions

The following remain to be formally established:

1. A complete grammar for all expressions.
2. A precise definition of division-expression equality.
3. Evaluation semantics for arbitrary nested division.
4. Termination of the rewrite system.
5. Confluence of the rewrite system.
6. Uniqueness of normal forms.
7. The relationship between structured division and ordinary algebraic equality.
8. Generalization from \mathbb R to arbitrary commutative rings.
9. Formal comparison with wheels and meadow-based systems.
