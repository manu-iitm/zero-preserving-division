References

This bibliography contains the principal literature relevant to the proposed division calculus.

Division by Zero, Wheels, and Totalized Division

[1] Carlström — Wheels

Jesper Carlström.

“Wheels — On Division by Zero.”

Mathematical Structures in Computer Science, 14(1), 143–184, 2004.

DOI: 10.1017/S0960129503004110

This paper develops the theory of wheels, algebraic structures in which division is defined more generally, including division by zero.

---

[2] Bergstra, Hirshfeld & Tucker — Meadows

J. A. Bergstra, Y. Hirshfeld, and J. V. Tucker.

“Meadows and the Equational Specification of Division.”

Theoretical Computer Science, 410, 1261–1271, 2009.

DOI: 10.1016/j.tcs.2008.12.015

This work develops meadows as algebraic structures with a totalized inverse operation, conventionally satisfying

[
0^{-1}=0.
]

---

[3] Bergstra & Middelburg — Non-Involutive Meadows

J. A. Bergstra and C. A. Middelburg.

“Division by Zero in Non-Involutive Meadows.”

Journal of Applied Logic, 13(1), 1–12, 2015.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jal.2014.10.001

This work investigates totalized division systems in which the inverse operation need not satisfy the usual involution property and includes approaches where division by zero differs from the standard meadow convention.

---

[4] Bergstra & Ponse — Common Meadows

J. A. Bergstra and J. V. Ponse.

“Division by Zero in Common Meadows.”

2015. 

Common meadows introduce an absorptive error value for exceptional computations and provide another algebraic approach to totalized division.

---

Idempotent Extensions and Division by Zero

[5] Sibrin & Orlov — Nonliquent Math

S. Sibrin and A. Orlov.

“Nonliquent Math.”

Preprint, 2024–2025.

This work is relevant to the present project because it considers an algebraic construction involving an idempotent element associated with formal division by zero, including structures of the form

[
\mathbb R\oplus\mathfrak i\mathbb R
]

with

[
\mathfrak i^2=\mathfrak i.
]

The existence of such constructions means that an idempotent extension associated with 1/0 should not itself be presented as a novel idea.

The present project differs in its proposed treatment of multiplication by zero and, especially, in its syntax-sensitive treatment of zero factors occurring inside denominators.

---

Scope of Literature Review

The above references are intended as an initial bibliography rather than a complete survey.

Before making any strong claim of mathematical novelty, the project should investigate additional literature concerning:

- wheels and generalized fields;
- meadows and totalized inverses;
- non-involutive meadows;
- common meadows;
- partial algebras;
- term-rewriting systems;
- algebraic semantics of division;
- zero divisors and idempotent extensions;
- formal treatments of undefined or exceptional arithmetic;
- and recent work on division by zero.

The novelty claim should ultimately be based on a formal comparison between the proposed calculus and the closest existing systems.
